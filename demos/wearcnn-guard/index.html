WearCNN-Guard: depression screening from one week of wrist movement

Load a week of minute-by-minute wrist activity. A 1-D CNN trained on 8,723 adults (NHANES) gives one of three answers, with a 90% conformal guarantee. Everything runs in your browser: your file is never uploaded.

Research demo, not a medical test. This tool cannot diagnose depression. It was trained on research-grade wrist sensors, and results from consumer smartwatches have not been validated. If you are struggling, please talk to a doctor, a mental-health professional or someone you trust. In an emergency, contact your local emergency services.

How to use it

  1. Try a sample first: click one of the three sample buttons below to see how it works.
  2. Prepare your file: a .csv with two columns, timestamp and activity, one row per minute: 
    timestamp,activity
2026-01-05 00:00,0
2026-01-05 00:01,12.5
2026-01-05 00:02,340
  3. How much data: at least 4 days, each with 10+ hours of wear (up to 7 days are used). Minutes with no data can be left out.
  4. What "activity" means: minute-level activity counts from a wrist accelerometer (for example ActiGraph, the device used in NHANES). Exports from research devices work best. Step counts or other watch scores are a different scale, so the result will be less reliable, and the page will warn you.
  5. Drop the file in the box below, or click to choose it. You'll see which days were usable, the activity pattern, and the answer.

1. Which days were usable

A day counts if the tracker was worn for at least 10 hours. Runs of 60+ minutes of zero movement are treated as "not worn".

2. Your average day

Average activity (log scale) in 10-minute steps, midnight to midnight. The shaded band shows how much your days varied. These two curves are exactly what the CNN reads.

3. The answer

Model score (probability of a PHQ-9 score of 10 or more):

The score alone is not the answer. Conformal prediction turns it into a set of possible labels that, across people, includes the true one at least 90% of the time, for depressed and non-depressed people separately. If both labels stay in the set, the honest answer is "not sure". In testing, about 1 in 3 people got a confident single answer.

How it works

The 1-D CNN (3 convolution layers, written from scratch in NumPy, ported here to JavaScript) was trained on 6,978 NHANES 2011–2014 adults. The answer threshold was calibrated on 1,745 different people, separately for each class (Mondrian conformal prediction, α = 0.1). Accuracy is modest (AUC ≈ 0.65), because movement is only one part of mental health. This is why the model says "not sure" for most people instead of guessing.
