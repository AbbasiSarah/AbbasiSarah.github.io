Almost every famous AI model you use, from chatbots to image generators, was trained on **Nvidia chips**. So when the person who led Nvidia for more than 30 years suddenly leaves, the whole AI world pays attention. That happened this week.

## What happened

On **12 November 2026**, Nvidia's board announced that co-founder **Jensen Huang** had resigned, **effective immediately**, as CEO, president and board member. He had led the company since he helped start it in **1993**.

- **Colette Kress**, Nvidia's chief financial officer since **2013**, is now **interim CEO** (a temporary boss) while staying CFO.
- **Harvey C. Jones**, a former CEO of chip-design company Synopsys, became Nvidia's **first-ever board chair**.
- The board is searching for a permanent CEO and will look at people **inside and outside** the company.

The official announcement **gave no reason** for the resignation. It came a few days after the board opened an **independent investigation** on **8 November** into whether Huang had been **targeted by a Chinese intelligence operation** through a personal relationship. Huang admitted in a letter to staff that he had been unfaithful to his wife and said he would cooperate. Nvidia said it had **no reason to believe** company secrets or national security were harmed, and China's foreign ministry called the spying claims groundless. The investigation's findings have **not been made public**, so the rest is still unconfirmed.

Nvidia's shares closed about **4% lower** on the day of the announcement. (This isn't financial advice.)

## Why it matters

Nvidia makes **GPUs** (graphics processing units), chips that can do thousands of small calculations at once. That makes them perfect for training AI. Big AI labs, cloud companies and startups all depend on them.

Analysts quoted by Fox Business called Kress a steady and trusted leader, but worried about losing Huang's global deal-making. One analyst pointed out that Nvidia has been **led by its founder since day one**, with many big decisions resting on one person. That's what makes the change risky.

The first big test comes soon: Nvidia reports its quarterly results on **17 November**, Kress's first chance to reassure investors and customers.

## What we can learn

1. **Hardware powers AI.** We talk about models a lot, but chips are the engine. Learning a little about GPUs and how they speed up training is worth your time.
2. **Don't build everything around one person.** In companies and in team projects, sharing knowledge and planning for "what if someone leaves" keeps work going.
3. **Trust is part of tech.** In the AI era, leaders' choices can become security and business questions overnight.
4. **Women lead tech too.** Colette Kress now runs one of the world's most important AI companies. Finance, operations and strategy are real paths into tech leadership, not just coding.
