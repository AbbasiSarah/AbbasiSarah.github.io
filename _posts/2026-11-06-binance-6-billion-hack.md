On 29 October 2026, hackers drained about **$6.3 billion** from **Binance**, the world's largest crypto exchange. It is the biggest crypto theft ever recorded, larger than the $1.5 billion Bybit hack of 2025.

## What happened

- The attack ran across **ten blockchains**, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, Tron and the XRP Ledger.
- In the first **four minutes**, the attackers moved about 233 million XRP out of five of Binance's *hot wallets* (wallets that stay connected to the internet so customers can withdraw quickly).
- Binance froze withdrawals, but the last stolen transfer still went out **nearly four hours** after the first.
- About **$3 billion** was frozen quickly, mostly stablecoins like USDT and USDC, which their issuers can block. Coins like Bitcoin have no central issuer, so nobody can freeze them.
- Binance says customers will not lose money. It is covering the losses, partly from its emergency insurance fund (SAFU).

## Who did it

The FBI and the blockchain-analysis firms **TRM Labs** and **Chainalysis** blame **TraderTraitor**, a North Korean group linked to the Lazarus Group. Binance said it had already blocked 57 suspected North Korea-linked attacks this year.

## How they got in

This is the part every developer should read. According to Binance, the attack started **in June**:

1. An engineer on the wallet team searched online for a popular **AI coding tool** and downloaded an installer that hackers had tampered with.
2. The attackers then used his stolen login details to get into a **second wallet developer's** computer.
3. From there, they waited, studied the system, and finally ran a script that sent the money out.

The hackers didn't break any encryption, and no wallet keys leaked. They tricked one person into running the wrong file. Reuters also reported that fake job interviews were part of the campaign, a trick North Korean groups use often.

## What we can learn

- **Download tools only from the official site** or a trusted package manager, never from a search ad or a random link. That goes double for AI tools, because everyone is rushing to try them.
- **One laptop shouldn't be able to empty a wallet.** Big transfers need several people to approve them and automatic limits.
- **Attackers are patient.** This attack sat quietly for about four months before anyone noticed.
