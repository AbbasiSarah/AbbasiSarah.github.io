For two years, everyone has asked whether AI is a bubble. In late October 2026, Reuters said that in **China**, the bubble has **already burst**.

## The numbers

- China's **STAR50 index**, which tracks its leading AI and semiconductor companies, fell **about 40% in under four months**. That wiped out roughly **$480 billion** in market value.
- On **22 October**, the **CSI 300** (China's 300 biggest listed companies) entered a **bear market**, meaning a fall of at least 20% from its peak. Bloomberg reported that Chinese stocks had lost **$2.4 trillion** since May.
- Some star names fell hardest. The robot maker **Unitree** has lost about **half its value** since its much-hyped stock market debut in August, and chipmaker **CXMT** dropped more than a quarter in a month.

Why does one sector hit the whole market so hard? According to Goldman Sachs, AI stocks make up about **42% of China's market value** and 57% of trading. When AI falls, almost everyone's savings fall with it.

## Why it happened

There wasn't one single trigger. Reuters and Bloomberg point to several things at once:

1. **Doubts about profits.** Companies spent heavily on chips and data centres, but it isn't clear that customers will pay enough for AI products to cover the cost.
2. **Export limits.** US tariffs, a possible US ban on Chinese data-centre equipment, and limits on selling Chinese AI products such as humanoid robots abroad shrink the market these companies hoped to sell into.
3. **Speculation.** Many ordinary investors bought in at the peak. One Beijing fund manager told Reuters the market had turned into a *speculative, crazy bull*.
4. **Weak spending at home.** Chinese consumers have been cutting back, which hurts the wider market too.

## Is AI itself failing?

No. A falling stock price isn't the same as failing technology. Chinese AI labs are still building strong models. **DeepSeek** just raised about $15 billion, and **Huawei** borrowed $2.3 billion to buy more AI chips. What burst was the **price people were willing to pay** for AI companies, not AI.

History agrees: after the dot-com crash in 2000, many companies disappeared, but the internet kept growing.

## What it means for the rest of us

- **Watch the US.** American AI stocks face the same question: will the huge spending pay off? Many analysts see China as an early warning.
- **For learners and job seekers:** bubbles hit stock prices first. Real skills, such as building, testing and deploying models, stay valuable when the hype cools.
- **For investors:** if one theme makes up nearly half a market, that market isn't diversified. *(This isn't financial advice.)*
